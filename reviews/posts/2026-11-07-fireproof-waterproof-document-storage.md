Ask anyone who has rebuilt after a flood or fire what they wish they'd done differently, and many will say the same thing: "I wish I'd known where my papers were." Insurance policies, IDs, deeds and medical records are boring until the day you need them, and then they're everything.

The good news: protecting them is one of the easiest preparedness steps there is. It takes an afternoon and one good container.

## Why documents matter so much

[Ready.gov's financial preparedness page](https://www.ready.gov/financial-preparedness) puts it plainly: after a disaster, having access to personal financial, insurance, medical and other records is crucial for starting the recovery process quickly. The [Ready.gov basic kit list](https://www.ready.gov/kit) recommends keeping copies of insurance policies, identification and bank account records saved electronically or in a waterproof, portable container. (StashAndSet isn't affiliated with FEMA or Ready.gov; we just think their guidance is a sensible place to start.)

## What to keep protected

- Driver's licenses, passports and birth certificates
- Social Security cards
- Insurance policies (home, flood, auto, health, life)
- Deeds, titles and leases
- Wills, powers of attorney and medical directives
- Medical records, prescriptions and vaccination records (including pets)
- A list of bank and credit accounts and key phone numbers
- Some emergency cash
- Small valuables and irreplaceable photos

## Fireproof vs. waterproof: know the difference

These are two different protections, and many products offer one more than the other.

- **Fire resistance** is about keeping the inside cool enough that paper doesn't char. Look at the temperature and duration a product is rated for, and read the manufacturer's specs.
- **Water resistance** is about keeping out rain, firefighting water or floodwater. In Florida, water is often the bigger threat.

For important papers, look for something that addresses both. And for extra insurance, slip documents into zip-top bags inside the container.

## An afternoon project

1. Gather your documents into one pile.
2. Scan or photograph each one and save copies to the cloud and a drive.
3. Put originals (or copies, for things you need daily) in your fireproof, waterproof container.
4. Add some cash and your list of important phone numbers.
5. Store it somewhere everyone in the house knows, close to your go-bag.
6. Set a reminder to update it once a year.
