*Use it every day. Rely on it when it counts.*

When we started StashAndSet, we needed a way to decide what belonged in the store and what didn't. There are endless "survival" products out there, and plenty of them are gimmicks. So we picked a neutral, sensible starting point: the [Ready.gov basic disaster supplies kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit). (StashAndSet isn't affiliated with FEMA or Ready.gov; we just think their guidance is a sensible place to start.)

We already have a [full checklist post](https://stashandset.com/blogs/news/ready-gov-emergency-kit-checklist-everyday-edition) that walks through the list item by item. This post is different. It's about *why* we use Ready.gov as our filter, and how each part of our catalog lines up with it.

## Why Ready.gov, and not a prepper forum?

- **It's practical.** Ready.gov focuses on the basics that matter in common emergencies: storms, outages, evacuations.
- **It's calm.** No doomsday scenarios. Just water, food, light, information, first aid and documents.
- **It's universal.** It works whether you live in a Jacksonville apartment or a farmhouse.

Ready.gov also says your kit should be maintained: store food in a cool, dry place, replace expired items and rethink your needs every year. That fits our philosophy perfectly. If something is part of your everyday life, it gets checked and refreshed naturally.

## Our one extra rule: it has to be useful on a normal day

Ready.gov tells you *what* to have. We add one filter on top: would you use this on an ordinary Tuesday? If the answer is no, it probably ends up forgotten in a closet. Here's how that plays out, category by category.

## What we leave out

Using Ready.gov as a filter also tells us what *not* to sell. No tactical gimmicks, no fear-based "bug-out" gadgets, nothing you'd be embarrassed to leave on the counter. If it doesn't map to a real need, or you wouldn't use it on a normal day, it doesn't make the cut.

## Build it at your own pace

You don't need the whole list at once. Pick one category that feels thin in your home, maybe light or documents, and start there.
